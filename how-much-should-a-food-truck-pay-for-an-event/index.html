How much should a food truck pay for an event?

There's no single right booth fee, but trade groups have published some useful lines, and they all come back to one test: after the organizer's cut, food, staff and fuel, does the day leave you enough?

What the trade guidance says

  • Commission: 10% or less is fair, and never over 15%. Events taking more than 10% should offer a minimum sales guarantee. A flat fee should not go over $300 a day, and an event should charge a flat fee or a percentage, never both. (Washington State Food Truck Association)
  • Sales should be at least four times what you pay the organizer. The UK caterers' association calls this the "Times Four" rule.
  • If you need more than 80% of your expected sales just to break even, say no; under 60% is comfortable. (KitchenNmbrs)

Turning that into a number for your truck

The most you can pay depends on your own costs, so work it backwards: pick the sales you'd hit on a slow version of the event (60% of a normal day is a good stand-in) and find the fee that still lets you break even there.

The example truck in this guide: $14 average order; a slow, normal and busy day of 90, 150 and 210 orders ($1,260, $2,100 and $2,940 in sales); food and packaging 30% of sales; card fees 3%; one helper at $18 an hour for 9 hours (6 serving, 3 for travel and setup); $60 fuel and propane; $20 other; and no prepped food thrown away. For the example truck, a normal day at this event is $2,100 in sales, so 60% of that is $1,260. At $1,260 the truck covers food, card fees, the helper, fuel and other costs and has $602 left. That's the most it could pay as a flat fee and still break even on a slow day. As a commission with no flat fee, the ceiling works out to about 48%.

Those are walk-away numbers, not opening offers. The guidance above says a fair deal is far lower: $300 flat, or 10%. Ask for the fair number; know your limit.

Quick check before you sign

  1. Ask for last year's real attendance and how many food vendors are booked.
  2. Estimate a slow, normal and busy day in orders, not just "a good crowd".
  3. Check break-even against the normal day (under 60%?), and sales against the fee (4× or more?).
  4. Make sure a slow day doesn't cost you money.
