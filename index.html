How the lights work

  • Break-even under 60% of a normal day: go. Over 80%: say no. From KitchenNmbrs' guide "How do I know if an event or festival is worth it financially". The room between 60% and 80% is for a bad turnout, rain, or a line that moves slower than you hoped.
  • Sales should be at least 4× what you pay the organizer. The UK caterers' association NCASS calls this the "Times Four" rule.
  • Commission: 10% or less is fair; never over 15%; over 10% should come with a sales guarantee; a flat fee should stay under US$300 a day. From the Washington State Food Truck Association's event guidance.
  • A slow day should not cost you money, and the event should beat a normal day at your usual spot if you gave a number for that.

These are rules of thumb from the trade, not advice. You know your truck; the numbers just make the guess honest.

Questions

Is it really free?

Yes. The calculator, the counter-offer message and a log of your first 3 events are free. Nothing is uploaded and there's no account: everything stays in this browser. Pro is a one-time payment for trucks that do a lot of events and want the full season log.

Which number should I use for food cost?

Your food and packaging cost as a share of what you charge. If a $12 plate costs you $3.60 in food and boxes, that's 30%. Event menus are often smaller and pricier; use the event menu.

What's "whichever is higher"?

Some events charge, say, "$150 or 15% of sales, whichever is higher". Pick that option and the calculator charges you the bigger of the two at each sales level.

What's a sales guarantee?

Some organizers promise a minimum: if you sell less than, say, $1,500, they pay you the difference. The calculator counts that top-up.
