Flat fee, percentage, or "whichever is higher"?

Event organizers charge food trucks in three common ways: a flat booth fee, a percentage of sales, or "a flat fee or a percentage, whichever is higher". They feel similar on paper and land very differently depending on how the day goes.

The example truck in this guide: $14 average order; a slow, normal and busy day of 90, 150 and 210 orders ($1,260, $2,100 and $2,940 in sales); food and packaging 30% of sales; card fees 3%; one helper at $18 an hour for 9 hours (6 serving, 3 for travel and setup); $60 fuel and propane; $20 other. What the table shows

  • A percentage protects you on a slow day. At 15% the organizer only takes $189 when sales are $1,260, so a quiet day hurts less than a $250 flat fee.
  • A flat fee pays you more on a busy day. At $2,940 in sales, 15% is $441, while the flat fee stays $250. If you expect a big crowd, a flat fee is worth more to you.
  • "Whichever is higher" gives you the worst of both. On a slow day you pay the $200 floor; on a busy day you pay the full 15%. You never get the upside of a flat fee.
  • Flat fee plus a percentage is the most expensive version. The Washington State Food Truck Association says events should charge one or the other, never both.

Which to push for

If you trust the crowd (a repeat event with real attendance numbers), push for a flat fee. If it's a first-year event or the weather is a coin flip, push for a percentage at 10% or less, or ask for a minimum sales guarantee. If they insist on "whichever is higher", check that a slow day still leaves you something.
