How to counter an event's booth fee (with an email you can copy)

Most event fees are a first offer. Organizers want good trucks, and a short, friendly reply with numbers behind it often gets a better deal, or at least the information you need to decide.

Before you reply, know three numbers

  1. Your break-even at their terms (see how to work it out).
  2. Your walk-away fee: the most you could pay and still break even on a slow day.
  3. A fair ask: trade guidance says 10% commission or less, a flat fee of $300 a day or less, and never both (Washington State Food Truck Association).

Email template

Hi [name], thanks for the invite to [event]. We'd love to be there. We ran the numbers on [their terms, e.g. $350 flat + 25% of sales], and at the turnout we expect it doesn't quite work for a truck our size. Could you do [a flat fee of $300 with no commission], or [10% of sales with no flat fee], or the current terms with a minimum sales guarantee of [your break-even]? Also, could you share last year's real attendance and how many food vendors you're booking? Thank you! [your name, truck name]

Why this works

  • It offers options instead of a flat no. The organizer can pick the one that suits their budget.
  • A sales guarantee means the organizer tops you up if sales fall short. If they won't share any of the risk, that tells you something about their confidence in the crowd.
  • Attendance and vendor count are the two numbers that decide your sales. An organizer who can't give them is asking you to guess.
