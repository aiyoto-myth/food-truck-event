How many orders do you need to break even at an event?

Break-even is the sales level where the event stops costing you money. Below it you'd have been better off staying home; above it, every order is yours.

The formula

Split your costs into two kinds:

  • Fixed for the day: the flat booth fee, staff wages, fuel and propane, permits, ice, parking.
  • A share of every sale: food and packaging, card fees, and the organizer's commission.

Then: break-even sales = fixed costs ÷ (1 − the shares). Divide by your average order to get the number of orders.

The example truck in this guide: $14 average order; a slow, normal and busy day of 90, 150 and 210 orders ($1,260, $2,100 and $2,940 in sales); food and packaging 30% of sales; card fees 3%; one helper at $18 an hour for 9 hours (6 serving, 3 for travel and setup); $60 fuel and propane; $20 other; and no prepped food thrown away. Swap in your own numbers in the free calculator.

Worked example: $150 flat plus 10% commission

  • Fixed costs: $150 fee + $162 helper + $60 fuel + $20 other = $392
  • Shares of each sale: 30% food + 3% card + 10% commission = 43%, so you keep 57 cents of every dollar
  • Break-even sales: $392 ÷ 0.57 = $688, which is 50 orders at $14

A normal day at this event is $2,100, so break-even is only a third of it. That's a comfortable "go" by the usual rule: under 60% of expected sales is safe, over 80% is a no (KitchenNmbrs).

Two things people forget

  • Your line has a speed limit. If you can serve 25 orders an hour for 6 hours, you can't sell more than 150 orders however big the crowd is. Cap the busy day at what your window can do.
  • Your own hours count. A day with 3 hours of driving and setup on top of 6 serving is a 9-hour day. Divide what you keep by 9, not 6, to see what the event pays you per hour.
